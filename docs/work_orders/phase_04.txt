# Ratiocinatus Phase 4 Work Order

## Speaker-Attributed Utterance Corpus

**Phase:** 4
**Target application version:** 0.6.0
**Starting point:** Completed Phase 3 participant-speech evidence boundary
**Primary goal:** Produce a stable, source-addressed corpus of speaker-attributed utterances suitable for later discourse, claim, argument, and adjudication work.

---

## 1. Objective

Phase 4 shall consolidate Phase 2 transcript evidence and Phase 3 speaker-participation evidence into stable utterance records.

The phase must determine:

* which words belong together as one utterance;
* which speaker or speaker hypothesis is associated with that utterance;
* where utterances begin and end;
* how interruptions, restarts, repairs, fragments, and incomplete speech are represented;
* when spoken material quotes, paraphrases, reads, imitates, or attributes another voice;
* how local conversational context is assembled;
* how later transcript or identity corrections propagate;
* and how reviewers inspect and revise utterance construction without altering the source evidence.

An utterance is a structured analytical unit over transcript and speaker evidence.

It is not necessarily:

* a grammatical sentence;
* a complete thought;
* one transcript segment;
* one diarization turn;
* one uninterrupted interval;
* or one proposition.

Phase 4 must preserve the difference between what was audibly produced, how the transcript represents it, who appears to have produced it, and how the system groups it for later analysis.

---

## 2. Governing distinctions

The implementation must preserve the following distinctions.

### 2.1 Transcript segment

A Phase 2 transcription artifact produced from a bounded audio interval.

Transcript segmentation may reflect model behavior rather than discourse structure.

### 2.2 Speaker turn

A Phase 3 interval during which one speaker appears to hold the primary speaking role.

A turn may contain multiple utterances.

### 2.3 Utterance

A bounded unit of speech attributed to one apparent speaker and constructed for discourse analysis.

An utterance may be:

* complete;
* incomplete;
* interrupted;
* resumed;
* repaired;
* fragmentary;
* non-lexical;
* quoted;
* jointly produced;
* or attribution-uncertain.

### 2.4 Sentence

A linguistic or orthographic unit.

An utterance may contain several sentences or less than one sentence.

### 2.5 Proposition

A claim-like semantic unit that may later be extracted from an utterance.

Phase 4 must not equate utterances with claims or arguments.

### 2.6 Speaker attribution

A relationship between an utterance and Phase 3 participant evidence.

Attribution may be:

* machine-clustered;
* identity-hypothesized;
* manually bound;
* role-labeled;
* conflicting;
* or unknown.

### 2.7 Quoted speaker

A person whose words are represented inside another person’s utterance.

The quoted speaker is not the acoustic speaker of the current interval.

---

## 3. Governing principles

### 3.1 Prior evidence remains immutable

Phase 4 must not alter:

* Phase 1 source media or timing;
* Phase 2 transcript observations, words, alternatives, or correction history;
* Phase 3 observations, turns, clusters, hypotheses, or binding history.

Phase 4 may assemble derived utterance views from declared prior-phase versions.

### 3.2 Utterance construction must be reversible

Every utterance must retain enough lineage to reconstruct:

* the source audio interval;
* contributing transcript segments and words;
* contributing speaker turns and observations;
* corrections applied;
* segmentation rules used;
* and any manual review decisions.

### 3.3 No forced completeness

The system must not invent missing words, punctuation, syntactic completion, or intended meaning to make an utterance appear complete.

Incomplete speech must remain incomplete.

### 3.4 No forced speaker certainty

An utterance may remain attributed to:

* a local speaker cluster;
* an unresolved participant;
* multiple competing identities;
* or no assignable speaker.

Stable utterance construction must not require a real-world name.

### 3.5 Surface speech and interpretation remain separate

Phase 4 may identify structural phenomena such as interruption, restart, quotation, or repair.

It must not determine:

* whether a speaker is truthful;
* what the speaker ultimately believes;
* whether an answer is evasive;
* whether an argument is valid;
* or whether an utterance is persuasive.

## 4. Required scope

## 4.1 Utterance-segmentation policy

Define a versioned utterance-segmentation policy.

The policy must consider, where evidence permits:

* speaker continuity;
* pauses;
* transcript punctuation;
* syntactic completion;
* discourse markers;
* semantic continuity;
* interruption;
* overlap;
* restart;
* self-repair;
* moderator intervention;
* completion after interruption;
* transcript-segment boundaries;
* and source interval continuity.

The policy must define:

* hard segmentation conditions;
* soft segmentation indicators;
* prohibited merge conditions;
* maximum gap permitted inside one utterance;
* treatment of filler words;
* treatment of non-lexical vocalizations;
* treatment of uncertain speaker changes;
* treatment of cross-chunk continuity;
* and conditions requiring human review.

The segmentation policy must be deterministic over the same evidence and configuration.

---

## 4.2 Stable utterance records

Create stable utterance records.

Each utterance must include, at minimum:

* stable utterance identifier;
* utterance-corpus identifier;
* source corpus lineage;
* Phase 2 transcript-view version;
* Phase 3 identity-view version;
* source-media interval or ordered interval set;
* normalized-audio interval or ordered interval set;
* contributing transcript segments;
* contributing transcript words;
* contributing speaker turns;
* attributed speaker target;
* attribution status;
* attribution confidence and origin;
* displayed text;
* verbatim or normalized text distinction;
* utterance completeness classification;
* interruption status;
* repair status;
* overlap status;
* quotation status;
* context references;
* review status;
* creation process;
* format and policy versions;
* and integrity fields.

Identifiers must remain stable under identical evidence, configuration, and review state.

The implementation must define when:

* an utterance retains its identifier;
* a new version is produced;
* a successor utterance is created;
* or an utterance becomes invalidated.

---

## 4.3 Utterance text views

The system must distinguish multiple text views where applicable.

Required views include:

* raw machine transcript text;
* current corrected transcript text;
* utterance display text;
* minimally normalized analysis text;
* and review markup.

Normalization may include documented handling of:

* whitespace;
* punctuation;
* capitalization;
* repeated filler tokens;
* disfluency notation;
* and subtitle-specific line breaks.

Normalization must not:

* add omitted content;
* silently replace uncertain words;
* convert paraphrase into quotation;
* remove meaningful repetitions;
* erase interruption evidence;
* or alter the original transcript artifact.

Every normalized text field must declare its derivation policy.

---

## 4.4 Utterance completeness

Each utterance must receive an explicit completeness classification.

Required classifications should include equivalents of:

* complete;
* grammatically incomplete but discourse-complete;
* interrupted;
* abandoned;
* trailing off;
* clipped by source boundary;
* clipped by processing boundary;
* transcription incomplete;
* inaudible ending;
* resumed later;
* fragment;
* non-lexical;
* and unknown.

Completeness must not be inferred solely from punctuation.

The system must retain the evidence supporting the classification.

---

## 4.5 Interrupted speech

Represent interruption as a relationship among utterances and speaker activity.

Each interruption record must include:

* interrupted utterance;
* interruption onset;
* interrupting speaker or unresolved speaker;
* interrupting utterance where constructed;
* overlap interval;
* whether the original speaker continues underneath;
* whether the original utterance resumes;
* temporal evidence;
* confidence;
* and review status.

The system must distinguish:

* actual simultaneous interruption;
* immediate turn takeover;
* supportive interjection;
* backchannel;
* moderator cutoff;
* technical cutoff;
* audience interruption;
* and uncertain interruption.

Phase 4 must not assign blame or intent.

---

## 4.6 Resumed and continued utterances

Support utterances that continue after an interruption or temporary break.

A continuation relationship must record:

* predecessor utterance;
* successor utterance;
* intervening speech or event;
* elapsed gap;
* speaker consistency;
* lexical or syntactic continuation evidence;
* semantic continuation evidence where used;
* confidence;
* and disposition.

The system must distinguish:

* same utterance resumed;
* new utterance on the same topic;
* repeated restart;
* recap after interruption;
* and unresolved continuation.

Non-contiguous source intervals may belong to one logical utterance only when the policy explicitly permits this and preserves all component intervals.

A presentation view may join resumed components, but the underlying temporal pieces must remain visible.

---

## 4.7 Turn repair

Implement bounded turn-repair operations.

Turn repair addresses mismatches among:

* diarization turns;
* transcript segments;
* words;
* speaker boundaries;
* and utterance boundaries.

Supported repair proposals must include:

* split turn;
* merge adjacent turns;
* move boundary;
* reassign transcript words;
* detach incorrectly associated words;
* assign previously unattributed words;
* preserve mixed-speaker interval;
* and mark unresolved.

Every repair proposal must record:

* affected artifacts;
* proposed change;
* evidence basis;
* contrary evidence;
* confidence;
* whether it is automated or manual;
* and disposition.

Accepted repairs must produce derived successor records.

They must not rewrite Phase 2 or Phase 3 source artifacts.

---

## 4.8 Self-repair and speech disfluency

Represent audible repairs within a speaker’s own speech.

Relevant phenomena include:

* false start;
* restart;
* repetition;
* substitution;
* insertion;
* deletion-like abandonment;
* hesitation;
* filler;
* stutter-like repetition where audibly represented;
* and explicit correction.

A self-repair record should identify, where possible:

* reparandum, or abandoned material;
* interruption point;
* repair marker;
* repair text;
* relation between original and repaired material;
* source intervals;
* confidence;
* and review status.

Phase 4 must not diagnose medical, cognitive, or psychological conditions from disfluency.

Self-repair evidence must remain available to later analysis without requiring the utterance text to be artificially cleaned.

---

## 4.9 Overlap-aware utterance construction

Utterance segmentation must preserve Phase 3 overlap evidence.

Required behavior:

* permit simultaneous utterances;
* preserve independent speaker timelines;
* avoid serializing overlap into a false sequence;
* identify dominant and secondary speech only when supported;
* retain unattributed overlapping words;
* support partially recoverable speech;
* and surface overlap for review.

When transcript evidence cannot reliably separate overlapping speakers, the system must represent:

* mixed transcript evidence;
* uncertain word attribution;
* or untranscribed overlap.

It must not assign all words to the louder or currently selected speaker without explicit evidence.

---

## 4.10 Quotation handling

Create explicit spoken-quotation records.

The system must distinguish:

* direct quotation;
* partial quotation;
* paraphrase;
* attributed proposition;
* reported speech;
* reading from a document;
* recitation;
* imitation or impersonation;
* hypothetical speech;
* self-quotation;
* and uncertain quotation.

Each quotation record must include:

* quoting utterance;
* quoted span;
* quotation type;
* attributed quoted speaker where stated;
* attribution text;
* source of attribution;
* whether the quoted words are acoustically present only through the current speaker;
* whether an external source match exists;
* confidence;
* and review status.

A quoted person must not become the acoustic speaker attribution for the utterance.

Quotation marks inserted by a transcript model must not be treated as sufficient proof of quotation.

---

## 4.11 Embedded recordings and played media

The system must represent speech played inside the source recording.

Examples include:

* video clips;
* archival recordings;
* advertisements;
* remote feeds;
* voice mail;
* translated playback;
* and public-address audio.

Such speech must be distinguishable from a physically present participant.

The utterance record must be able to mark:

* primary-source participant speech;
* embedded-media speech;
* remote participant speech;
* synthesized speech;
* replayed speech;
* and uncertain source type.

Phase 4 need not identify the embedded speaker definitively, but it must avoid silently clustering played audio with present participants when evidence indicates a different source condition.

---

## 4.12 Speaker-attributed transcript assembly

Produce a canonical speaker-attributed transcript view.

Each rendered utterance should expose, as applicable:

* utterance identifier;
* source timestamp;
* speaker cluster or participant label;
* attribution status;
* utterance text;
* interruption or continuation markers;
* overlap markers;
* quotation markers;
* uncertainty markers;
* and review status.

The system must support at least:

* machine speaker-cluster view;
* reviewed participant-identity view;
* unknown-preserving view;
* correction-aware view;
* overlap-expanded view;
* and compact reading view.

No single rendered transcript replaces the underlying utterance corpus.

---

## 4.13 Context windows

Create deterministic context windows for later analytical consumers.

Context windows must be derived from utterance order and declared policy.

Required window types should include equivalents of:

* preceding utterances;
* following utterances;
* same-speaker history;
* current turn neighborhood;
* exchange window;
* question-response window where structurally known;
* interruption neighborhood;
* quotation neighborhood;
* and bounded temporal window.

Each context-window record must include:

* target utterance;
* included utterances;
* ordering;
* source intervals;
* inclusion reason;
* token or character count;
* truncation status;
* omitted-neighbor summary where applicable;
* policy version;
* and integrity status.

Context windows must not silently reorder overlapping speech into a false sequence.

Where simultaneous utterances exist, the window must preserve temporal relationships.

---

## 4.14 Context budgeting

Provide bounded context construction suitable for later language-model or analytical processing.

The implementation must support:

* maximum utterance count;
* maximum token estimate;
* maximum source duration;
* speaker-balanced selection where configured;
* recency weighting;
* question or interruption preservation;
* quotation-source preservation;
* and explicit truncation.

Truncation must be deterministic and reported.

The system must never imply that an analysis considered the complete exchange when the supplied context was truncated.

---

## 4.15 Correction propagation

Corrections from Phase 2 and Phase 3 must propagate through Phase 4 in a controlled and inspectable way.

Required behavior:

* detect changed transcript text;
* detect changed word boundaries;
* detect changed speaker turns;
* detect changed cluster membership;
* detect changed identity bindings;
* identify affected utterances;
* selectively invalidate affected utterance artifacts;
* preserve unaffected utterance identifiers where justified;
* rebuild dependent context windows;
* rebuild speaker-attributed transcript views;
* and record propagation results.

The system must distinguish:

* text-only changes;
* timing changes;
* speaker-attribution changes;
* segmentation changes;
* display-label changes;
* and source-lineage changes.

A participant display-name correction should not force utterance resegmentation.

A word-timing correction may require segmentation review if it crosses an utterance boundary.

---

## 4.16 Manual utterance review

Implement append-only manual review actions.

Supported actions must include:

* approve utterance;
* split utterance;
* merge utterances;
* move boundary;
* change completeness classification;
* mark interruption;
* remove incorrect interruption;
* link continuation;
* unlink continuation;
* mark quotation;
* revise quotation span;
* change quotation type;
* flag uncertain text;
* flag uncertain speaker attribution;
* and defer decision.

Each review action must include:

* review-action identifier;
* target artifact;
* prior state;
* proposed state;
* author;
* timestamp;
* rationale;
* evidence references;
* confidence or reviewer certainty;
* and resulting utterance-view version.

Manual review must not erase the machine-generated utterance proposal.

---

## 4.17 Review queues and review tools

Provide review-oriented tools for locating likely utterance problems.

Required review queues should include:

* low-confidence segmentation;
* uncertain speaker boundary;
* transcript word crossing speaker boundary;
* long utterance;
* very short fragment;
* unresolved interruption;
* unresolved continuation;
* overlap with uncertain attribution;
* likely turn-repair candidate;
* probable self-repair;
* uncertain quotation;
* conflicting speaker attribution;
* correction-affected utterance;
* and integrity-warning queue.

Review interfaces may initially be CLI and report based, but must provide:

* source interval;
* playable media reference or extraction command;
* local transcript context;
* speaker evidence;
* proposed action;
* competing alternatives;
* and current review status.

A reviewer must be able to inspect the evidence without reconstructing lineage manually.

---

Produce a portable Phase 4 corpus.

The export must include:

* corpus manifest;

* self-repair records;

The corpus must not require provider execution merely to inspect previously completed evidence.

## 6. Processing sequence

### Stage 1: Contracts and policy

* Define utterance and relationship contracts.
* Define segmentation policy.
* Define identity-view and transcript-view dependencies.
* Define identifier and versioning rules.
* Define correction-propagation boundaries.
* Define evaluation policy.

### Stage 2: Initial utterance segmentation

* Align Phase 2 words and segments with Phase 3 turns.
* Produce provisional utterance boundaries.
* Preserve uncertain boundaries.
* Create utterance components and text views.
* Validate source lineage.

### Stage 3: Completeness and disfluency

* Classify complete and incomplete utterances.
* Detect bounded restart, repetition, and self-repair candidates.
* Preserve raw wording.
* Produce review flags.

### Stage 4: Interruption and overlap

* Construct interruption relationships.
* Link interrupting and interrupted utterances.
* Preserve overlap timelines.
* Identify possible resumptions.
* Avoid assigning intent or blame.

### Stage 5: Turn repair

* Detect transcript and diarization boundary conflicts.
* Produce bounded repair proposals.
* Apply accepted repairs through successor artifacts.
* Preserve original evidence.

### Stage 6: Quotation and embedded speech

* Identify bounded quotation candidates.
* Distinguish acoustic speaker from quoted speaker.
* Represent played or remote speech.
* Produce quotation review queues.

### Stage 7: Speaker-attributed transcript

* Assemble machine-cluster and reviewed-identity views.
* Preserve unknown speakers.
* Render interruption, overlap, continuation, and quotation markers.
* Validate complete lineage.

### Stage 8: Context windows

* Implement deterministic window policies.
* Preserve temporal relations and overlap.
* Enforce context budgets.
* Record truncation and exclusions.

### Stage 9: Correction propagation and review

* Detect prior-phase changes.
* Selectively invalidate affected utterances.
* Rebuild dependent views and windows.
* Implement append-only review actions.
* Produce propagation reports.

### Stage 10: Evaluation and qualification

* Evaluate segmentation, attribution, interruption, quotation, and correction behavior.
* Exercise recovery and corruption cases.
* Qualify long-recording operation.
* Produce checked-in completion evidence.

Phase 4 may create structural evidence later consumed by those phases, but it must not silently perform them.

## 19. Exit gates

Phase 4 is complete only when all of the following gates pass.

### Gate 1: Stable utterance construction

The system produces stable utterance records from declared Phase 2 and Phase 3 views.

### Gate 2: Complete source lineage

Every utterance maps to its source intervals, transcript components, and speaker evidence.

### Gate 3: Speaker attribution

Each utterance carries an explicit machine, reviewed, conflicting, or unknown speaker-attribution state.

### Gate 4: Utterance completeness

Complete, incomplete, interrupted, abandoned, clipped, fragmentary, and unknown speech states are machine-readable.

### Gate 5: Turn repair

Transcript and diarization boundary conflicts can produce bounded, reviewable repair proposals without rewriting prior-phase evidence.

### Gate 6: Interrupted speech

Interruptions are represented through temporal relationships and overlap evidence without assigning intent or blame.

### Gate 7: Continued and resumed speech

The system can preserve bounded continuation relationships across interruption or pause without hiding non-contiguous source intervals.

### Gate 8: Self-repair and disfluency

False starts, repetitions, repairs, fillers, and abandoned material can be preserved without artificial cleanup.

### Gate 9: Overlap-aware corpus

Simultaneous utterances remain independently represented and are not flattened into a false sequence.

### Gate 10: Quotation handling

The system distinguishes acoustic speaker, quoting speaker, quoted speaker, quotation, paraphrase, and reported speech.

### Gate 11: Speaker-attributed transcript

Machine-cluster, reviewed-identity, and unknown-preserving transcript views can be rendered from the utterance corpus.

### Gate 12: Context windows

Deterministic, bounded, source-traceable context windows can be generated with explicit truncation.

### Gate 13: Correction propagation

Phase 2 and Phase 3 changes selectively invalidate and rebuild affected Phase 4 artifacts while preserving unaffected evidence.

### Gate 14: Manual review

Reviewers can split, merge, reclassify, relink, and defer utterance decisions through append-only records.

### Gate 15: Evaluation

Segmentation, attribution, interruption, continuation, quotation, overlap, and propagation behavior are evaluated against controlled references.

### Gate 16: Cache and recovery

Validated artifacts are reused safely, corrupted entries are quarantined, and interrupted processing resumes from persisted boundaries.

### Gate 17: Integrity

Malformed, incompatible, impossible, stale, or conflicting Phase 4 artifacts are detected and refused or explicitly degraded.

### Gate 18: Long-recording operation

A recording longer than two hours is processed with bounded memory, stable cross-chunk utterances, resumability, cache replay, export, and reload.

### Gate 19: Regression and boundary

All prior-phase tests pass, and no claim extraction, argument analysis, factual adjudication, credibility scoring, intent inference, or participant judgment is introduced.

## 20. Success definition

Phase 4 succeeds when Ratiocinatus can take compatible Phase 2 transcript evidence and Phase 3 participant-speech evidence and produce a portable, integrity-checked corpus of speaker-attributed utterances that:

* groups speech into stable analytical units;
* preserves source and prior-phase lineage;
* represents complete and incomplete speech;
* preserves interruption, overlap, continuation, restart, and repair;
* distinguishes quotation from acoustic speaker attribution;
* supports machine, reviewed, conflicting, and unknown speaker states;
* produces deterministic speaker-attributed transcript views;
* creates bounded context windows;
* propagates prior-phase corrections selectively;
* supports append-only human review;
* survives interruption and recovery;
* qualifies long-recording operation;
* and remains suitable for later discourse, claim, argument, and adjudication phases.

The resulting utterance corpus must improve analytical addressability without converting uncertain speech, uncertain attribution, or incomplete language into false certainty.
