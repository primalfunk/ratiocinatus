# Ratiocinatus Phase 2 Work Order

## Transcription and Temporal Speech Evidence

**Phase:** 2
**Target application version:** 0.4.0
**Date:** 2026-07-26
**Starting point:** Completed Phase 1 audiovisual corpus boundary
**Primary goal:** Create a timestamped, confidence-bearing transcript whose relationship to the underlying audio remains explicit, inspectable, correctable, and reproducible.

---

## 1. Objective

Phase 2 shall transform the normalized and addressable Phase 1 audio corpus into structured temporal speech evidence.

The phase must establish:

* where speech probably occurs;
* what words or utterances may have been spoken;
* when those words or utterances occurred;
* how confident the system is in each result;
* where the evidence is weak, ambiguous, missing, or contradictory;
* what alternative transcript candidates were considered;
* what corrections were later introduced;
* and how every transcript claim maps back to the Phase 1 corpus.

The transcript is not an authoritative reconstruction of speech.

It is a versioned analytical artifact composed of evidence-bearing hypotheses over addressable source intervals.

Phase 2 must not perform speaker identity resolution, argument reconstruction, factual adjudication, credibility assessment, rhetorical analysis, scoring, or judgment.

---

## 2. Governing principles

### 2.1 The source remains authoritative

The Phase 1 corpus remains the authoritative audiovisual source.

No transcript text, subtitle, model response, corrected text, or derived representation may replace or modify the source corpus.

Every speech or transcript artifact must retain a valid reference to the source audio interval from which it was derived.

### 2.2 Speech detection and transcription are separate claims

The system must distinguish:

* evidence that speech is present;
* a proposed transcription of that speech;
* timing assigned to transcript segments;
* timing assigned to individual words;
* confidence in speech presence;
* confidence in the proposed text;
* and confidence in temporal alignment.

A transcription must not imply that speech detection, text recognition, and temporal alignment were equally certain.

### 2.3 Unknown is a valid result

The system must be able to represent:

* no speech detected;
* probable speech with no reliable transcription;
* partially intelligible speech;
* uncertain word boundaries;
* uncertain segment boundaries;
* overlapping or competing transcript interpretations;
* non-lexical vocalization;
* inaudible content;
* unintelligible content;
* model refusal or failure;
* and unsupported language or audio conditions.

The system must not invent text merely to eliminate an unknown region.

### 2.4 Corrections preserve history

Corrections must not silently overwrite prior transcript evidence.

The original candidate, its provenance, its confidence, the correction, the correction source, the author or process responsible, and the reason for the correction must remain recoverable.

### 2.5 Model output is evidence, not truth

Model-generated text must remain attributable to:

* provider;
* model;
* model version or fingerprint where available;
* decoding configuration;
* prompt or task configuration where applicable;
* source interval;
* processing chunk;
* run identifier;
* and timestamp.

Unsupported claims of model determinism are prohibited.

## 3. Required scope

Phase 2 must implement the following capabilities.

### 3.1 Speech activity evidence

Implement speech activity detection over the normalized Phase 1 audio derivative.

Required behavior:

* consume Phase 1 source and chunk addressing;
* support complete-recording and chunked processing;
* preserve overlap ownership rules inherited from Phase 1;
* represent speech and non-speech intervals;
* represent uncertain boundary regions;
* associate confidence with each interval or boundary;
* prevent duplicate speech intervals at chunk overlaps;
* retain raw provider output where permitted and practical;
* and support reproducible reconstruction of the final speech activity timeline.

Speech activity detection must distinguish, where supported:

* probable speech;
* probable non-speech;
* uncertain activity;
* non-lexical human vocalization;
* music or noise likely to interfere with detection;
* and provider failure.

The implementation may use one initial speech activity provider, but it must define a provider-independent interface.

### 3.2 Transcription provider abstraction

Implement a provider-independent transcription interface.

At minimum, the interface must support:

* model and capability inspection;
* language configuration;
* automatic language proposal where supported;
* segment transcription;
* word timestamp requests;
* alternative candidate requests where supported;
* confidence or probability output where supported;
* bounded decoding configuration;
* provider timeout;
* cancellation at supported boundaries;
* structured failure reporting;
* and raw response retention or normalized evidence capture.

A local transcription provider is preferred for the initial implementation.

Provider-specific fields must not leak into authoritative domain contracts except through an explicit extension or raw-evidence field.

### 3.3 Transcript segments

Create stable transcript-segment records.

Each segment must include, at minimum:

* stable segment identifier;
* corpus identifier;
* selected audio-stream lineage;
* source interval;
* normalized-audio interval;
* processing-chunk lineage;
* proposed transcript text;
* normalized text, where applicable;
* language claim;
* speech activity evidence references;
* transcription provider and model provenance;
* text confidence;
* timing confidence;
* segment-boundary confidence;
* alternative candidate references;
* low-confidence classifications;
* creation timestamp;
* format and policy versions;
* and integrity fields.

Segment identifiers must be stable under identical source, configuration, provider result, and segmentation policy.

The implementation must define what changes invalidate a segment identifier or require a new transcript artifact version.

### 3.4 Word-level evidence

Where provider capability permits, create word-level transcript evidence.

Each word record must include:

* stable word identifier;
* parent segment identifier;
* source interval;
* normalized-audio interval;
* token or surface text;
* normalized form, where applicable;
* sequence position;
* recognition confidence where available;
* timing confidence;
* boundary uncertainty;
* provider token references where retained;
* and provenance.

The system must not fabricate word-level confidence when a provider supplies only segment-level confidence.

The system must not imply exact word boundaries when timestamps are estimated, interpolated, or alignment-derived.

Estimated, provider-native, and externally aligned timestamps must be explicitly distinguished.

### 3.5 Temporal alignment and source mapping

All transcript intervals must map through the Phase 1 temporal model.

The implementation must preserve:

* integer-microsecond time representation;
* source timeline;
* normalized derivative timeline;
* chunk-local timeline;
* overlap ownership;
* clipping classifications;
* discontinuity classifications;
* and source-to-derivative mapping tolerances.

The system must validate that:

* word intervals remain inside their parent segment unless explicitly classified otherwise;
* segment intervals remain inside the addressable corpus;
* transcript order does not regress without an explicit discontinuity;
* overlap ownership prevents duplicated canonical evidence;
* and subtitle timestamps remain consistent with canonical transcript intervals.

Rounding for display or subtitle formats must not alter canonical microsecond values.

### 3.6 Low-confidence regions

Create explicit low-confidence-region records.

A low-confidence region may arise from:

* low speech probability;
* low transcription confidence;
* low temporal-alignment confidence;
* disagreement among candidates;
* probable overlap;
* clipping;
* noise;
* music;
* reverberation;
* distortion;
* unsupported language;
* rapid speech;
* incomplete words at boundaries;
* provider error;
* missing output;
* or failed validation.

Each record must include:

* source interval;
* classification;
* severity;
* evidence references;
* threshold or policy responsible;
* whether review is recommended;
* and whether the region blocks downstream use.

Low confidence must not be represented solely through formatting, color, or prose.

It must be machine-readable.

### 3.7 Alternative transcript candidates

Support alternative transcript candidates without treating all alternatives as equally plausible.

Alternative candidates may originate from:

* provider-native alternatives;
* multiple decoding passes;
* multiple models;
* different language assumptions;
* different segmentation policies;
* or later bounded reprocessing.

Each candidate must include:

* candidate identifier;
* parent speech interval;
* proposed text;
* provider and run provenance;
* score or rank where available;
* comparison basis;
* relationship to the selected candidate;
* and selection or rejection reason.

The system must distinguish:

* selected candidate;
* unselected alternative;
* superseded candidate;
* invalid candidate;
* and unresolved candidate set.

Candidate selection must be explainable from recorded policy and evidence.

A lower-ranked candidate must not be deleted merely because another candidate was selected.

### 3.8 Correction records

Implement append-only transcript correction records.

Corrections must support:

* replacement;
* insertion;
* deletion;
* split;
* merge;
* boundary adjustment;
* language correction;
* normalization-only correction;
* uncertainty annotation;
* and restoration of an earlier candidate.

Each correction must record:

* correction identifier;
* target artifact and version;
* prior value;
* proposed value;
* correction type;
* affected source interval;
* author or process;
* timestamp;
* reason;
* evidence or review reference;
* and resulting transcript version.

Corrections must not alter the Phase 1 corpus or erase the original model output.

The system must be able to render:

* original machine transcript;
* current corrected transcript;
* correction history;
* and a difference report.

Human corrections and automated corrections must be explicitly distinguished.

### 3.9 Transcript assembly

Assemble canonical transcript views from validated segment and word evidence.

Required views:

* machine transcript;
* corrected transcript;
* segment timeline;
* word timeline where available;
* low-confidence review transcript;
* alternative-candidate view;
* and correction-history view.

Transcript assembly must be deterministic over the same stored artifacts and policy version.

Assembly must not silently resolve:

* overlapping segments;
* temporal gaps;
* contradictory candidates;
* invalid word ordering;
* or unresolved corrections.

Such conditions must produce explicit validation findings.

### 3.10 Subtitle export

Implement subtitle export from canonical transcript evidence.

Required initial formats:

* WebVTT;
* SubRip Text, or SRT.

Subtitle export must:

* derive from a declared transcript version;
* preserve source references in export metadata or companion manifest;
* identify whether text is machine-only or corrected;
* use explicit timestamp-rounding rules;
* prevent invalid or regressive timestamps;
* handle long cues through documented segmentation policy;
* represent uncertain or unavailable text conservatively;
* avoid silently removing low-confidence cues;
* and produce a validation report.

Subtitle files are presentation derivatives, not authoritative transcript storage.

Loss introduced by subtitle format constraints must be documented in an export manifest.

### 3.11 Transcription evaluation

Create a controlled transcription evaluation framework.

The framework must support, at minimum:

* reference transcript ingestion;
* reference-to-source interval mapping;
* word error rate;
* character error rate;
* segment timing error;
* word timing error where references permit;
* speech activity precision;
* speech activity recall;
* speech activity F1;
* missed-speech duration;
* false-speech duration;
* low-confidence calibration or reliability analysis;
* candidate-selection evaluation;
* subtitle cue validity;
* and correction impact.

Evaluation must report both aggregate and stratified results.

Useful strata include:

* clean speech;
* noise;
* music;
* overlapping speech;
* rapid speech;
* quiet speech;
* clipped boundaries;
* accented speech where represented in the controlled corpus;
* non-speech vocalization;
* and long-recording chunk boundaries.

The implementation must not claim general transcription quality from a single clean fixture.

### 3.12 Cache, resume, and recovery

Phase 2 must extend the Phase 1 operational guarantees.

Required behavior:

* cache speech activity outputs separately from transcription outputs;
* cache provider results by source, interval, model, configuration, and policy;
* validate cached artifacts before reuse;
* quarantine corrupt cache entries;
* invalidate only affected downstream artifacts where practical;
* resume after persisted processing boundaries;
* recover from interruption between speech detection, transcription, alignment, assembly, correction application, and export;
* and avoid retranscribing validated intervals unnecessarily.

The system must demonstrate recovery after interruption at each material persisted stage.

A provider or process failure in one chunk must not corrupt completed evidence from unrelated chunks.

### 3.13 Reporting

Produce both machine-readable and human-readable Phase 2 reports.

Required reports:

* speech activity report;
* transcription report;
* word-timing report;
* low-confidence report;
* alternative-candidate report;
* correction-history report;
* subtitle-export report;
* transcription-evaluation report;
* cache and recovery report;
* provider capability report;
* transcript integrity report;
* and normalized phase-completion report.

Reports must separate:

* measured results;
* provider claims;
* inferred classifications;
* configured thresholds;
* validation findings;
* and unresolved limitations.

## 5. Processing sequence

The implementation should proceed in the following order.

### Stage 1: Contracts and provider boundaries

* Define Phase 2 domain contracts.
* Define speech activity and transcription provider interfaces.
* Define provider capability inspection.
* Define temporal-confidence and text-confidence semantics.
* Define transcript and correction versioning rules.
* Define invalidation and cache keys.

### Stage 2: Speech activity

* Process normalized Phase 1 audio.
* Produce speech, non-speech, and uncertain intervals.
* Reconcile chunk overlaps.
* Validate source mappings.
* Produce speech activity reports.

### Stage 3: Segment transcription

* Transcribe validated speech intervals.
* Preserve raw or normalized provider evidence.
* Produce segment-level text and confidence.
* Represent failures and unintelligible intervals explicitly.
* Validate temporal ordering and containment.

### Stage 4: Word timing

* Capture provider-native word timestamps where available.
* Introduce alignment only through an explicit provider or processing stage.
* Classify timestamp origin.
* Validate word ordering and segment containment.
* Record uncertain boundaries.

### Stage 5: Alternatives and confidence

* Capture or generate bounded alternatives.
* Define candidate ranking and selection.
* Create low-confidence regions.
* Produce review-oriented views.

### Stage 6: Corrections and transcript versions

* Implement append-only correction records.
* Assemble original and corrected transcript versions.
* Produce correction diffs.
* Validate correction lineage.

### Stage 7: Subtitle export

* Export WebVTT and SRT.
* Validate cues and timestamps.
* Produce export manifests.
* Demonstrate machine-only and corrected transcript exports.

### Stage 8: Evaluation and qualification

* Evaluate transcription and speech activity.
* Test noisy, ambiguous, overlapping, clipped, and chunk-boundary cases.
* Test cache, resume, corruption, configuration changes, and provider failures.
* Produce checked-in qualification evidence.

## 6. Required command-line operations

Provide CLI operations equivalent to:

* inspect transcription-provider capabilities;
* detect speech activity;
* transcribe a corpus;
* resume transcription;
* validate transcript artifacts;
* render a transcript;
* list low-confidence regions;
* inspect transcript alternatives;
* apply or import corrections;
* show correction history;
* export WebVTT;
* export SRT;
* evaluate against a reference transcript;
* produce Phase 2 reports;
* and verify a complete Phase 2 corpus.

Commands must produce structured error output suitable for automation.

CLI rendering must not be the sole representation of any required artifact.

## 7. Configuration requirements

Configuration must be explicit and persistable.

At minimum, record:

* speech activity provider;
* transcription provider;
* provider model;
* provider version or fingerprint where available;
* language mode;
* speech thresholds;
* segmentation policy;
* decoding parameters;
* candidate count;
* word-timestamp policy;
* alignment policy;
* confidence thresholds;
* low-confidence classifications;
* chunk and overlap behavior;
* timeout policy;
* cache policy;
* correction policy;
* subtitle segmentation policy;
* timestamp-rounding policy;
* and evaluation normalization policy.

Changing a configuration field that can affect evidence must invalidate or version the relevant artifact.

Configuration fields that affect only rendering must not force unnecessary retranscription.

---

## 8. Confidence requirements

Confidence must be modeled conservatively.

The system must not collapse all confidence into one number.

At minimum, distinguish:

* speech-presence confidence;
* transcript-text confidence;
* segment-timing confidence;
* word-timing confidence;
* boundary confidence;
* candidate-selection confidence;
* and correction status.

When a provider does not supply a meaningful confidence measure, the field must remain unavailable or explicitly derived.

Provider scores from different models must not be treated as directly comparable without a documented calibration method.

Thresholds must be recorded with the resulting artifacts.

---

## 9. Integrity requirements

Phase 2 integrity validation must detect at least:

* missing Phase 1 corpus references;
* source hash mismatch;
* invalid source intervals;
* invalid derivative intervals;
* segment time regression;
* word time regression;
* words outside parent segments;
* duplicate canonical intervals caused by chunk overlap;
* malformed Unicode;
* impossible confidence values;
* absent provider provenance;
* invalid candidate selection;
* correction cycles;
* correction against an unknown version;
* unresolved conflicting corrections;
* subtitle timestamp regression;
* subtitle cues outside the source duration;
* unsupported schema or policy versions;
* corrupted cached output;
* and transcript assembly from incompatible lineages.

Integrity failure must be explicit and must not silently yield a partially trusted transcript.

---

## 10. Qualification corpus

Phase 2 qualification must use more than one transcription condition.

At minimum, include controlled cases covering:

* clean single-speaker speech;
* silence;
* background noise;
* music under speech;
* non-speech vocalization;
* rapid speech;
* quiet speech;
* clipped speech at interval boundaries;
* ambiguous words;
* overlapping voices;
* speech spanning processing chunks;
* false-positive speech-like noise;
* provider timeout or failure;
* malformed provider output;
* missing word timestamps;
* alternative candidates;
* and human correction.

The existing Riverton corpus may remain part of qualification, but it must not be the sole basis for transcription-quality claims unless it demonstrably contains the required conditions and independently prepared reference transcripts.

Any synthetic or generated qualification material must be labeled as such.

Reference transcripts must retain provenance and must not be generated solely by the same model being evaluated.

---

## 11. Required negative proofs

The qualification suite must demonstrate explicit refusal, degradation, or failure handling for:

* unsupported audio or corpus version;
* missing normalized audio derivative;
* corrupted audio evidence;
* provider unavailable;
* model unavailable;
* provider timeout;
* malformed provider result;
* timestamps outside the requested interval;
* invalid confidence values;
* word timestamps in reverse order;
* duplicated overlap output;
* incompatible transcript and corpus identifiers;
* invalid correction target;
* conflicting correction history;
* invalid subtitle timing;
* unsupported subtitle export version;
* and incomplete cached artifacts.

A negative case passes only when the system produces the expected typed failure or conservative degraded result.

A crash, silent omission, invented transcript, or unrecorded fallback is not a passing negative proof.

---

## 12. Regression requirements

Phase 2 must preserve all Phase 0, Phase 0.5, and Phase 1 guarantees.

The regression suite must demonstrate that Phase 2 does not:

* alter the source audiovisual files;
* alter normalized Phase 1 audio;
* alter Phase 1 stream selection;
* alter Phase 1 temporal mappings;
* alter Phase 1 chunk ownership;
* invalidate valid Phase 1 corpus exports;
* introduce speaker identity artifacts;
* introduce argument or judgment artifacts;
* introduce factual claims about the content;
* or convert transcript hypotheses into authoritative source records.

Existing Phase 0 and Phase 1 qualification evidence must remain valid.

---

## 13. Explicit exclusions

Phase 2 must not implement:

* speaker diarization;
* speaker naming;
* voiceprint identity;
* face-to-voice association;
* character or participant identity;
* argument extraction;
* claim extraction;
* evidence weighting;
* credibility analysis;
* contradiction adjudication;
* sentiment analysis;
* rhetorical analysis;
* legal or factual conclusions;
* judgment;
* scoring of speakers or statements;
* audiovisual enhancement presented as source evidence;
* source repair;
* translation as canonical transcript;
* or generative completion of inaudible speech.

Experimental internal diagnostics may exist only when clearly separated from persisted Phase 2 domain artifacts and excluded from the success definition.

## 16. Exit gates

Phase 2 is complete only when all of the following gates pass.

### Gate 1: Stable source mapping

Every canonical transcript segment maps validly to the Phase 1 corpus and normalized audio timeline.

### Gate 2: Speech activity evidence

Speech, non-speech, and uncertain regions are represented with explicit provenance and confidence.

### Gate 3: Timestamped transcript

The system produces segment-level transcript text with stable identifiers, source intervals, provenance, and confidence.

### Gate 4: Word evidence

Word-level timestamps are stored where supported, with timestamp origin and uncertainty explicitly represented.

### Gate 5: Unknown and low confidence

Weak, missing, or ambiguous evidence produces machine-readable low-confidence or unknown records rather than invented certainty.

### Gate 6: Alternatives

Alternative transcript candidates are preserved, ranked where possible, and linked to explicit selection decisions.

### Gate 7: Corrections

Corrections are append-only, versioned, attributable, reversible through history, and incapable of erasing the original model evidence.

### Gate 8: Subtitle export

Valid WebVTT and SRT derivatives can be generated from declared transcript versions with documented rounding and loss.

### Gate 9: Evaluation

Speech activity, text accuracy, timing accuracy, confidence behavior, and subtitle validity are evaluated against controlled references.

### Gate 10: Cache and recovery

Validated outputs are reused safely, affected outputs are invalidated correctly, corrupted entries are quarantined, and interrupted processing resumes without duplicating or losing evidence.

### Gate 11: Integrity

Malformed, inconsistent, incompatible, or corrupted transcript artifacts are detected and refused.

### Gate 12: Long-recording operation

A recording longer than two hours is processed through the Phase 1 chunk boundary with bounded memory, overlap reconciliation, resumability, and valid final transcript assembly.

### Gate 13: Regression and boundary

All prior-phase tests pass, and no speaker identity, argument, judgment, scoring, or analytical interpretation artifact is introduced.

---

## 17. Success definition

Phase 2 succeeds when Ratiocinatus can take a valid Phase 1 audiovisual corpus and produce a portable, integrity-checked, timestamped transcript evidence package that:

* identifies probable speech intervals;
* records proposed transcript text;
* preserves segment and word timing;
* expresses uncertainty;
* retains alternatives;
* supports append-only correction;
* exports subtitles;
* survives interruption and recovery;
* supports controlled evaluation;
* and remains fully traceable to the underlying audiovisual source.

The result must be suitable as evidence input for later speaker, discourse, argument, and adjudication phases without presenting machine transcription as settled fact.
