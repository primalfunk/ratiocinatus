# Ratiocinatus Phase 5 Work Order

## Discourse-Act Construction

**Phase:** 5
**Target application version:** 0.7.0
**Starting point:** Completed Phase 4 speaker-attributed utterance corpus
**Primary goal:** Represent what each utterance is doing in the conversation through source-grounded, confidence-bearing discourse-act evidence.

---

## 1. Objective

Phase 5 shall transform the stable Phase 4 utterance corpus into a structured representation of conversational function.

The phase must determine, without forcing certainty:

* whether an utterance asserts something;
* asks a question;
* answers an earlier question;
* objects to a prior statement;
* rebuts an objection or competing position;
* concedes a point;
* qualifies, limits, or conditions a statement;
* defines a term;
* gives an example;
* quotes or attributes another statement;
* performs a procedural conversational act;
* or performs some combination of these functions.

Phase 5 must also establish:

* which words or utterance spans support each discourse-act proposal;
* which earlier or later discourse objects the act relates to;
* what alternative classifications remain plausible;
* how confident the system is;
* what evidence supports or weakens the classification;
* and how human review may revise the analysis without changing the Phase 4 utterance record.

A discourse act is an analytical claim about conversational function.

It is not:

* a factual truth determination;
* a complete semantic claim;
* an argument-validity judgment;
* an assessment of sincerity;
* a psychological interpretation;
* or a score of rhetorical quality.

---

## 2. Governing distinctions

The implementation must preserve the following distinctions.

### 2.1 Utterance

A Phase 4 source-grounded unit of speech.

An utterance is the primary input to Phase 5.

### 2.2 Discourse-act observation

A bounded proposal that all or part of an utterance performs a conversational function.

One utterance may produce:

* no classifiable act;
* one act;
* several simultaneous acts;
* or several sequential acts over different spans.

### 2.3 Discourse-act type

A controlled category such as assertion, question, concession, or procedural speech.

A type is not a complete description of meaning.

### 2.4 Discourse relation

A directed relationship between a discourse act and another utterance, act, question, proposition-like target, or procedural state.

Examples include:

* answers;
* objects to;
* rebuts;
* concedes;
* qualifies;
* defines;
* exemplifies;
* quotes;
* redirects;
* and requests clarification of.

### 2.5 Evidence span

The exact utterance text and source interval supporting a discourse-act proposal.

An evidence span must not be replaced by an unexplained whole-utterance label when only part of the utterance supports the classification.

### 2.6 Semantic claim

A later-phase proposition extracted from discourse content.

Phase 5 may identify an assertion act but must not yet construct a complete factual-claim system.

### 2.7 Argument role

A later analytical determination that a statement serves as a premise, conclusion, attack, support, or inferential bridge.

A rebuttal-like discourse act may later participate in an argument, but Phase 5 must not assume that every rebuttal is logically successful.

### 2.8 Illocutionary force and surface form

The grammatical form of an utterance and its conversational function may differ.

Examples:

* a declarative sentence may function as a question;
* a question may function as an objection;
* “Can we move on?” may be procedural rather than informational;
* “You really believe that?” may be both a question and a challenge.

The system must not classify solely from punctuation or syntax.

## 3. Governing principles

### 3.1 Phase 4 remains immutable

Phase 5 must not alter:

* Phase 1 audiovisual evidence;
* Phase 2 transcript evidence;
* Phase 3 speaker and identity evidence;
* Phase 4 utterance segmentation, text, attribution, or review history.

Phase 5 may derive discourse-act views only from declared Phase 4 corpus versions.

### 3.2 Multi-label classification is required

An utterance may perform more than one conversational function.

For example:

> “Yes, but that figure excludes rural counties.”

This may contain:

* an answer;
* a concession;
* an objection;
* and a qualification.

The implementation must not force one dominant label when evidence supports several compatible acts.

### 3.3 Alternatives remain visible

When several classifications are plausible, the system must retain:

* selected proposal;
* alternative proposals;
* evidence for each;
* evidence against each;
* confidence;
* and unresolved status.

### 3.4 Explicit relation targets are preferred

An objection, answer, rebuttal, concession, qualification, or definition should identify its target where evidence permits.

The system must distinguish:

* identified target;
* probable target;
* multiple candidate targets;
* implicit target;
* and unresolved target.

### 3.5 Unknown is valid

The system must support:

* unclassified utterance;
* mixed or ambiguous act;
* insufficient context;
* truncated act;
* unintelligible act;
* ceremonial or phatic speech;
* non-lexical speech;
* and unsupported act type.

### 3.6 Function is not merit

Phase 5 may identify that an utterance functions as an answer or rebuttal.

It must not determine that the answer is adequate, the rebuttal succeeds, or the objection is reasonable.

## 4. Required discourse-act vocabulary

The system must define a closed, versioned vocabulary.

At minimum, it must include the following families.

## 4.1 Assertive acts

Required types should include equivalents of:

* assertion;
* denial;
* affirmation;
* prediction;
* report;
* description;
* attribution;
* evaluation;
* correction;
* restatement;
* summary;
* and uncertainty statement.

An assertion classification must indicate that content is presented as holding, not that the content is true.

---

## 4.2 Question acts

Required types should include equivalents of:

* information question;
* yes-or-no question;
* alternative question;
* clarification question;
* confirmation question;
* challenge question;
* rhetorical question;
* follow-up question;
* procedural question;
* and embedded or quoted question.

The system must distinguish question form from question function.

---

## 4.3 Answer acts

Required types should include equivalents of:

* direct answer;
* partial answer;
* qualified answer;
* indirect answer;
* negative answer;
* affirmative answer;
* answer by correction;
* answer by rejection of premise;
* answer deferred;
* explicit refusal to answer;
* inability to answer;
* and candidate answer with unresolved target.

Phase 5 may represent the relationship between question and answer.

It must not yet score responsiveness or adequacy.

---

## 4.4 Objection and challenge acts

Required types should include equivalents of:

* objection;
* challenge;
* disagreement;
* counterexample proposal;
* premise challenge;
* relevance challenge;
* definition challenge;
* evidence challenge;
* procedural objection;
* and unsupported generalized challenge.

The classification must identify the challenged target where possible.

---

## 4.5 Rebuttal acts

Required types should include equivalents of:

* direct rebuttal;
* rebuttal by denial;
* rebuttal by counterevidence;
* rebuttal by counterexample;
* rebuttal by alternative explanation;
* rebuttal by qualification;
* rebuttal by scope correction;
* rebuttal by causal challenge;
* and purported rebuttal with unresolved target.

A rebuttal classification does not establish that the rebuttal defeats the target.

---

## 4.6 Concession acts

Required types should include equivalents of:

* full concession;
* partial concession;
* temporary concession;
* hypothetical concession;
* concession for argument;
* acknowledgment without agreement;
* and apparent concession with uncertain scope.

The system must identify what is conceded and what remains contested where text permits.

---

## 4.7 Qualification acts

Required types should include equivalents of:

* scope qualification;
* temporal qualification;
* conditional qualification;
* probabilistic qualification;
* exception;
* limitation;
* hedging;
* precision correction;
* category restriction;
* and threshold qualification.

Qualification must be represented as modifying another act or span where possible.

---

## 4.8 Definition acts

Required types should include equivalents of:

* explicit definition;
* operational definition;
* stipulative definition;
* lexical definition;
* category inclusion;
* category exclusion;
* distinction;
* term clarification;
* and definition challenge.

The system must preserve the defined term, defining span, and scope.

---

## 4.9 Example and illustration acts

Required types should include equivalents of:

* example;
* counterexample;
* analogy;
* illustration;
* anecdotal example;
* hypothetical example;
* case citation;
* enumeration;
* and example introduced but incomplete.

An example classification does not establish representativeness.

---

## 4.10 Quotation and attribution acts

Phase 5 must consume, but not replace, Phase 4 quotation evidence.

Required types should include equivalents of:

* direct quotation;
* partial quotation;
* paraphrase;
* reported speech;
* attributed position;
* self-quotation;
* citation of prior utterance;
* quotation challenge;
* quotation correction;
* and uncertain attribution.

The system must distinguish:

* acoustic speaker;
* quoting speaker;
* attributed speaker;
* and original source where known.

---

## 4.11 Procedural acts

Required types should include equivalents of:

* floor request;
* floor grant;
* floor denial;
* turn yield;
* time warning;
* time-expired notice;
* topic transition;
* agenda setting;
* request to answer;
* request to clarify;
* request to stop;
* moderator intervention;
* rule invocation;
* rule explanation;
* acknowledgment of procedure;
* introduction;
* closing;
* greeting;
* thanks;
* apology;
* and technical interruption notice.

Procedural acts should later support referee-oriented information without being converted into automatic violations or blame.

## 5. Required scope

## 5.1 Discourse-act provider abstraction

Implement a provider-independent analysis interface.

At minimum, the interface must support:

* one utterance plus bounded context;
* multiple act proposals;
* act spans;
* relation-target proposals;
* confidence or ranking;
* alternatives;
* structured output;
* model capability inspection;
* deterministic or reproducibility settings where supported;
* timeout;
* bounded retry;
* schema validation;
* raw-output retention or normalized evidence capture;
* and typed failure reporting.

The implementation may combine:

* deterministic rules;
* lexical patterns;
* syntactic analysis;
* local language models;
* or hybrid methods.

No single provider output may become authoritative without normalization and validation.

---

## 5.2 Discourse-act observations

Create stable discourse-act observation records.

Each observation must include, at minimum:

* stable observation identifier;
* Phase 4 utterance-corpus lineage;
* target utterance;
* evidence span;
* source-media interval;
* act family;
* act type;
* act modifiers;
* proposed relation target;
* relation-target confidence;
* classification confidence;
* confidence origin;
* analysis method;
* provider and model provenance where applicable;
* alternative candidates;
* contrary evidence;
* context-window reference;
* review status;
* creation timestamp;
* format and policy versions;
* and integrity fields.

Observations must be immutable.

Selections, corrections, and review decisions must create successor or decision records.

---

## 5.3 Evidence spans

Every selected discourse act must be supported by one or more explicit evidence spans.

An evidence span must include:

* utterance identifier;
* start and end text offsets;
* contributing word identifiers where available;
* source-media interval;
* exact displayed text;
* text-view version;
* span role;
* and confidence.

Span roles should include equivalents of:

* act trigger;
* act content;
* target reference;
* qualification marker;
* concession marker;
* negation;
* quotation;
* procedural formula;
* and contextual support.

The system must validate that text offsets and word references correspond to the declared Phase 4 text view.

---

## 5.4 Composite utterances

Support utterances containing multiple sequential or nested discourse acts.

Examples include:

* answer followed by objection;
* concession followed by rebuttal;
* definition followed by example;
* quotation followed by rejection;
* procedural request followed by substantive assertion;
* and assertion containing a qualification.

The implementation must support:

* multiple evidence spans;
* ordered subacts;
* nested acts where justified;
* shared text spans;
* and explicit act relationships.

A whole utterance must not be assigned one label when materially different spans perform distinct functions.

---

## 5.6 Question construction

Create question artifacts from question-like discourse acts.

Each question artifact must include:

* stable question identifier;
* source act;
* question span;
* question type;
* requested information or decision form where structurally recoverable;
* explicit alternatives where present;
* presupposition markers where detected;
* addressee or candidate addressees;
* procedural or substantive status;
* scope;
* confidence;
* and review status.

Phase 5 must not decide whether a question is fair, loaded, misleading, or answerable except through narrowly structural evidence and explicit uncertainty.

---

## 5.7 Answer relations

Link answer candidates to question artifacts.

Each answer relation must record:

* answer act;
* target question;
* answer form;
* explicitness;
* polarity where applicable;
* qualification;
* whether the answer rejects a presupposition;
* whether the answer is deferred or refused;
* alternative question targets;
* confidence;
* and review status.

The system must support:

* one answer responding to several questions;
* several utterances jointly answering one question;
* answer before an explicit question where context supports it;
* and unresolved answer target.

No adequacy, completeness, or evasion score is required in Phase 5.

---

## 5.8 Objection and rebuttal relations

Construct bounded objection and rebuttal relations.

Each relation must identify, where possible:

* target utterance or act;
* challenged span;
* challenge type;
* rebuttal method;
* supporting evidence span;
* qualification;
* alternative target;
* confidence;
* and unresolved issues.

The implementation must distinguish:

* objection to content;
* objection to evidence;
* objection to wording;
* objection to definition;
* objection to procedure;
* and objection to attribution.

---

## 5.9 Concession and qualification structures

Represent concessions and qualifications as modifiers of other discourse objects.

Each structure must include:

* conceding or qualifying act;
* target act or span;
* conceded content;
* retained disagreement where present;
* qualification dimension;
* scope;
* condition;
* exception;
* confidence;
* and review status.

The system must preserve cases such as:

> “That was true in 2022, but not after the law changed.”

This contains both temporal concession and temporal qualification.

---

## 5.10 Definition structures

Create definition records.

Each definition must include:

* defined expression;
* defining text;
* definition type;
* source act;
* scope;
* applicable context;
* explicit exclusions;
* competing definitions where present;
* definition challenge links;
* confidence;
* and review status.

Definitions must remain local to their declared discourse scope unless later evidence justifies broader reuse.

---

## 5.11 Example structures

Create example records.

Each example must include:

* source act;
* example span;
* generalization or target act where identified;
* example type;
* real, hypothetical, quoted, or uncertain status;
* temporal and participant references where structurally present;
* confidence;
* and review status.

Phase 5 must not determine whether an example proves a generalization.

---

## 5.12 Procedural-state representation

Create a limited procedural conversation state.

The representation may include:

* current recognized speaker;
* pending question;
* active response interval;
* moderator instruction;
* time warning;
* time expiration;
* granted extension;
* requested clarification;
* topic transition;
* and unresolved procedural event.

Every procedural state change must be linked to discourse acts and source intervals.

This state is descriptive.

It must not automatically assign violation, fault, or sanction.

---

## 5.13 Alternatives and candidate selection

Support multiple act candidates per utterance or span.

Each candidate set must record:

* candidate classifications;
* evidence spans;
* relation targets;
* confidence or rank;
* compatibility with other candidates;
* exclusion relationships;
* selection policy;
* selected candidates;
* rejected candidates;
* deferred candidates;
* and selection rationale.

Compatible acts may be selected together.

Mutually exclusive candidates must remain visible when unresolved.

---

## 5.14 Confidence model

Confidence must be multidimensional.

At minimum, distinguish:

* act-type confidence;
* evidence-span confidence;
* target-relation confidence;
* question-type confidence;
* answer-link confidence;
* quotation-use confidence;
* procedural-state confidence;
* and selection confidence.

The system must not fabricate provider confidence where unavailable.

Derived confidence must record:

* derivation method;
* source features;
* thresholds;
* and limitations.

Confidence from different providers must not be treated as directly comparable without calibration.

---

## 5.15 Correction propagation

Phase 5 must respond to Phase 4 corpus changes.

Required behavior:

* detect changed utterance text;
* detect changed utterance boundaries;
* detect changed speaker attribution;
* detect changed quotation structure;
* detect changed interruption or continuation relations;
* identify affected discourse acts;
* selectively invalidate affected observations;
* rebuild affected relation targets;
* preserve unaffected act identifiers where justified;
* rebuild procedural state;
* rebuild review queues;
* and record propagation results.

A display-label-only speaker change should not invalidate act classification unless identity-specific context was explicitly used.

A text or boundary change affecting an evidence span must invalidate that span and its dependent act.

## 7. Processing sequence

### Stage 1: Contracts and vocabulary

* Define the discourse-act vocabulary.
* Define act families and modifiers.
* Define relation types.
* Define evidence-span semantics.
* Define candidate and confidence models.
* Define identifier and versioning rules.
* Define correction-propagation boundaries.

### Stage 2: Deterministic baseline

* Implement high-precision lexical and structural rules.
* Detect clear questions, procedural formulas, explicit concessions, qualifications, definitions, examples, and quotations.
* Produce evidence spans.
* Preserve unknown results.
* Establish a measurable baseline before model assistance.

### Stage 3: Provider-assisted classification

* Analyze utterances with bounded Phase 4 context windows.
* Produce multiple candidate acts.
* Produce candidate evidence spans and targets.
* Validate structured output.
* Preserve provider provenance and failures.

### Stage 4: Candidate consolidation

* Combine deterministic and provider-assisted observations.
* Preserve agreement and disagreement.
* Select compatible acts.
* Retain unresolved alternatives.
* Produce canonical machine discourse acts.

### Stage 5: Question-answer construction

* Create question artifacts.
* Link answer candidates.
* Preserve deferred, refused, indirect, and presupposition-rejecting answers.
* Avoid adequacy scoring.

### Stage 6: Objection, rebuttal, concession, and qualification

* Link challenges to targets.
* Construct rebuttal relations.
* Identify conceded and retained positions.
* Identify qualification scope.
* Preserve target ambiguity.

### Stage 7: Definitions, examples, and quotations

* Construct definition records.
* Link examples to candidate generalizations.
* Consume Phase 4 quotation evidence.
* Distinguish quotation use from acoustic attribution.

### Stage 8: Procedural discourse

* Identify procedural acts.
* Build bounded procedural state.
* Represent moderator actions and timing-related speech.
* Do not assign violations or blame.

### Stage 9: Review and correction propagation

* Implement append-only review.
* Build review queues.
* Detect Phase 4 changes.
* Selectively rebuild affected acts and relations.
* Preserve unaffected artifacts.

### Stage 10: Evaluation and qualification

* Evaluate act types, evidence spans, relations, alternatives, and confidence.
* Exercise negative cases, recovery, and long-recording mechanics.
* Export and reload the discourse corpus.
* Produce checked-in completion evidence.

* missing Phase 4 lineage;
* incompatible utterance-corpus versions;
* source hash mismatch;
* discourse act targeting an unknown utterance;
* evidence span outside utterance text;
* word references outside the evidence span;
* source interval inconsistent with the span;
* unsupported act type;
* malformed act modifiers;
* duplicate canonical act selection;
* incompatible selected alternatives;
* relation targeting an unknown artifact;
* answer relation targeting a non-question without explicit justification;
* objection or rebuttal with impossible temporal target under the configured policy;
* concession with no conceded content;
* qualification with no target or scope;
* definition with no defined expression;
* example with invalid span;
* quotation use incompatible with Phase 4 quotation evidence;
* procedural-state cycle or impossible transition;
* confidence outside permitted range;
* missing confidence origin;
* review-history cycle;
* stale correction propagation;
* mixed corpus versions inside one discourse view;
* unsupported schema or policy versions;

Integrity failure must produce typed findings and must not silently generate a partially trusted discourse view.

## 12. Evaluation requirements

Create a controlled evaluation framework capable of measuring:

* act-family precision, recall, and F1;
* act-type precision, recall, and F1;
* multi-label exact match;
* multi-label partial match;
* evidence-span precision, recall, and overlap;
* relation-target accuracy;
* question-type accuracy;
* answer-link accuracy;
* objection-target accuracy;
* rebuttal-target accuracy;
* concession-scope accuracy;
* qualification-scope accuracy;
* definition-span accuracy;
* example-target accuracy;
* quotation-use accuracy;
* procedural-act accuracy;
* alternative-candidate recall;
* calibration or confidence reliability;
* unknown-state appropriateness;
* correction-propagation completeness;
* unaffected-artifact stability;
* and human-review impact.

Evaluation policy must specify:

* span-overlap thresholds;
* treatment of compatible multi-label acts;
* treatment of nested acts;
* treatment of unresolved targets;
* treatment of rhetorical questions;
* treatment of incomplete utterances;
* treatment of overlap;
* and treatment of human-reviewed references.

Results should be stratified by act family and conversational condition.

## 13. Required negative proofs

Qualification must demonstrate typed refusal or conservative degradation for:

* unsupported Phase 4 format;
* missing utterance corpus;
* corrupted utterance lineage;
* provider unavailable;
* model unavailable;
* provider timeout;
* malformed structured output;
* unsupported act type;
* evidence span outside the utterance;
* invalid word references;
* confidence outside allowed range;
* answer linked to unknown question;
* objection linked to unknown target;
* rebuttal relation cycle;
* concession without content;
* qualification without scope;
* definition without term;
* quotation incompatible with Phase 4 evidence;
* impossible procedural transition;
* incompatible selected alternatives;
* stale review action;
* correction propagation from the wrong utterance version;
* export with mixed corpus versions;
* unsupported schema version;
* and corrupted cache entry.

A crash, silent omission, fabricated target, forced classification, or destructive rewrite is not a passing negative proof.

## 14. Cache, resume, and recovery

Phase 5 must preserve stage-local recovery.

Required behavior:

* cache deterministic observations separately from provider observations;
* cache context-dependent provider output by exact context policy and input lineage;
* cache candidate consolidation separately from review decisions;
* cache relation construction separately from act classification;
* cache procedural-state assembly separately;
* validate artifacts before reuse;
* quarantine corrupt entries;
* selectively invalidate Phase 4 correction-affected artifacts;
* preserve unaffected act identifiers where justified;
* resume after every material persisted stage;
* and avoid provider reinvocation when valid observations remain reusable.

Recovery qualification must include interruption after:

* deterministic classification;
* provider analysis;
* evidence-span normalization;
* candidate consolidation;
* question construction;
* answer linking;
* objection and rebuttal linking;
* concession and qualification construction;
* definition and example construction;
* procedural-state assembly;
* review assembly;
* and export.

## 15. Long-recording requirements

Phase 5 must qualify operation on a recording longer than two hours.

The proof must demonstrate:

* bounded memory;
* incremental utterance processing;
* deterministic context-window retrieval;
* bounded relation-target search;
* cross-chunk conversational continuity where policy permits;
* no duplicate act ownership;
* interruption and resume;
* cache replay;
* portable export and reload;
* and complete integrity validation.

Synthetic marker acts may prove mechanics but must not support claims about natural discourse-act quality.

Phase 5 must preserve all Phase 0 through Phase 4 guarantees.

The regression suite must demonstrate that Phase 5 does not:

* alter source media;
* alter normalized audio;
* alter transcript evidence;
* alter diarization evidence;
* alter identity bindings;
* alter utterance segmentation;
* alter quotation evidence;
* force unknown speakers into identities;
* invent missing speech;
* treat assertion as truth;

* determine rebuttal success;
* determine argument validity;
* assign interruption blame;

* perform factual adjudication;
* score credibility;
* make psychological or clinical judgments;

## 20. Exit gates

Phase 5 is complete only when all of the following gates pass.

### Gate 1: Immutable discourse corpus

The system produces a versioned discourse corpus without modifying Phase 4 or earlier evidence.

### Gate 2: Stable discourse-act observations

Every act observation is stable, source-grounded, and linked to a declared utterance version.

### Gate 3: Controlled vocabulary

All selected acts use a closed, versioned discourse-act vocabulary.

### Gate 4: Multi-label representation

One utterance may contain multiple compatible or sequential discourse acts without forced single-label collapse.

### Gate 5: Evidence spans

Every selected act has valid, inspectable evidence spans linked to utterance text, words, and source intervals.

### Gate 6: Assertions

Assertive functions can be represented without implying truth.

### Gate 7: Questions

Questions can be classified by conversational function rather than punctuation alone.

### Gate 8: Answers

Answer candidates can be linked to questions with direct, indirect, partial, qualified, deferred, refused, and unresolved states.

### Gate 9: Objections

Objections and challenges can be represented with explicit or unresolved targets.

### Gate 10: Rebuttals

Rebuttal acts and methods can be represented without determining whether they succeed.

### Gate 11: Concessions

Full, partial, hypothetical, and scoped concessions can be represented with retained disagreement where present.

### Gate 12: Qualifications

Temporal, conditional, probabilistic, categorical, and scope qualifications can modify explicit targets.

### Gate 13: Definitions

Defined expressions and defining spans can be represented with local scope and competing definitions.

### Gate 14: Examples

Examples, counterexamples, analogies, and hypothetical cases can be linked to candidate targets without implying proof.

### Gate 15: Quotations

Quotation and attribution functions remain distinct from acoustic speaker identity and preserve Phase 4 quotation lineage.

### Gate 16: Procedural speech

Moderator instructions, timing notices, floor management, topic transitions, and other procedural acts can be represented without assigning violations or blame.

### Gate 17: Alternatives and confidence

Competing act classifications and targets remain inspectable with multidimensional confidence.

### Gate 18: Correction propagation

Phase 4 changes selectively invalidate and rebuild affected Phase 5 artifacts while preserving unaffected evidence.

### Gate 19: Append-only review

Reviewers can approve, reject, revise, add, relink, or defer discourse acts through attributable append-only records.

### Gate 20: Controlled evaluation

Act classification, evidence spans, relation targets, alternatives, confidence, and propagation are evaluated against controlled references.

### Gate 21: Cache and recovery

Validated artifacts are safely reused, corrupted entries are quarantined, and interrupted processing resumes from persisted boundaries.

### Gate 22: Portable export and integrity

The discourse corpus can be exported, reloaded, and completely validated without provider execution.

### Gate 23: Long-recording operation

A recording longer than two hours can be processed with bounded memory, bounded context, resumability, cache replay, export, and reload.

### Gate 24: Regression and boundary

All prior-phase tests pass, and no factual adjudication, argument-validity judgment, answer-quality score, intent inference, credibility score, or participant judgment is introduced.

## 21. Success definition

Phase 5 succeeds when Ratiocinatus can take a valid Phase 4 speaker-attributed utterance corpus and produce a portable, integrity-checked discourse representation that:

* identifies what conversational functions utterances appear to perform;
* supports assertions, questions, answers, objections, rebuttals, concessions, qualifications, definitions, examples, quotations, and procedural speech;
* permits multiple acts within one utterance;
* anchors each act to explicit evidence spans;
* links acts to their conversational targets;
* preserves alternatives and uncertainty;
* supports append-only human review;
* propagates prior-phase corrections selectively;
* survives interruption and recovery;
* supports controlled evaluation;
* and remains fully traceable to the audiovisual source.

The resulting discourse corpus must be suitable for later claim, proposition, argument, rule, and adjudication phases without treating conversational function as factual truth, argumentative success, speaker intent, or participant merit.
