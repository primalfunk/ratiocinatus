# Ratiocinatus Phase 3 Work Order

## Speaker Diarization and Participant Identity Foundations

**Phase:** 3
**Target application version:** 0.5.0
**Date:** 2026-07-26
**Starting point:** Completed Phase 2 timestamped transcript-evidence boundary
**Primary goal:** Determine who appears to be speaking across addressable source intervals without forcing speaker continuity or real-world identity certainty.

---

## 1. Objective

Phase 3 shall transform the Phase 1 audiovisual corpus and Phase 2 temporal speech evidence into structured speaker-participation evidence.

The phase must establish:

* where speaker turns begin and end;
* when more than one person appears to be speaking;
* which speech intervals appear to belong to the same voice;
* how consistent each proposed speaker cluster is;
* where speaker assignment remains uncertain;
* what identity hypotheses may be associated with a speaker cluster;
* how reference voices may support or weaken those hypotheses;
* how a human reviewer may bind, reject, revise, or leave identity unresolved;
* and how every diarization or identity claim maps back to the source recording.

Phase 3 must not assume that:

* one diarization cluster always represents one person;
* one person always maps to one cluster;
* a voice match proves legal or real-world identity;
* a visible participant is necessarily the current speaker;
* a transcript speaker label is correct merely because it is internally consistent;
* or every speech interval can be assigned confidently.

The result is a participant-identity foundation, not a final speaker-identification system.

---

## 2. Governing distinctions

The implementation must preserve the following distinctions throughout its contracts, processing, reports, and user-facing views.

### 2.1 Speech activity

A claim that human speech is probably present.

This is inherited from Phase 2 and must not be silently redefined by the diarization provider.

### 2.2 Speaker turn

A bounded interval during which one speaker appears to hold the primary speaking role.

A speaker turn is a temporal claim, not an identity claim.

### 2.3 Speaker observation

A bounded piece of acoustic evidence extracted from a source interval and attributed provisionally to a voice representation.

Observations may later be merged, split, rejected, or marked unusable.

### 2.4 Speaker cluster

A hypothesis that multiple observations probably originate from the same speaker.

A cluster is not a person.

### 2.5 Participant identity hypothesis

A proposition that a cluster or observation may correspond to a named or otherwise described participant.

A hypothesis must carry evidence, confidence, alternatives, and status.

### 2.6 Identity binding

An explicit decision associating a cluster or observation with a participant identity.

Bindings may be:

* manual;
* reference-supported;
* imported from trusted external metadata;
* provisional;
* confirmed for a bounded scope;
* rejected;
* superseded;
* or unresolved.

### 2.7 Unknown state

A valid result indicating that the available evidence does not justify speaker assignment, cluster continuity, or identity.

Unknown must never be treated as a processing failure merely because the system cannot name or cluster a speaker.

## 3. Evidentiary principles

### 3.1 The recording remains authoritative

Phase 1 audiovisual evidence remains authoritative.

Phase 2 transcripts, Phase 3 clusters, voice embeddings, identity hypotheses, and manual labels are analytical derivatives.

No Phase 3 operation may alter the recording, normalized audio, canonical Phase 2 transcript, or prior evidence lineage.

### 3.2 Diarization and identity are separate stages

The system must support useful diarization even when no real-world participant identity is known.

Speaker clustering must not depend on names being available.

Identity binding must not be required to complete diarization.

### 3.3 Similarity is not identity

Acoustic similarity may support a same-speaker or identity hypothesis, but it does not prove identity.

The system must preserve alternative explanations, including:

* similar voices;
* poor recording conditions;
* channel effects;
* compression artifacts;
* mimicry;
* synthesized or replayed voice;
* very short samples;
* overlapping speakers;
* emotional or physiological voice changes;
* and provider error.

### 3.4 Identity claims require scope

A binding must state what it applies to.

Possible scopes include:

* one observation;
* one speaker turn;
* one local segment;
* one Phase 3 cluster;
* one recording;
* one recording series;
* or a declared corpus.

A local identity decision must not silently become a global identity assertion.

### 3.5 Human decisions remain attributable

Manual identity binding, cluster merge, cluster split, rejection, and correction must be append-only and attributable.

The original machine proposal must remain recoverable.

### 3.6 Unknown and conflict are first-class states

The implementation must support:

* unidentified speaker;
* uncertain cluster assignment;
* unresolved cluster split;
* unresolved cluster merge;
* conflicting identity hypotheses;
* insufficient voice evidence;
* unusable reference voice;
* probable speaker change with uncertain boundary;
* and overlap with unassignable participants.

## 4. Required scope

Phase 3 must implement the following capabilities.

## 4.1 Diarization provider abstraction

Implement a provider-independent diarization interface.

At minimum, the interface must support:

* provider capability inspection;
* input interval declaration;
* speaker-count constraints where supported;
* minimum and maximum speaker proposals where supported;
* turn segmentation;
* overlapping-speech output where supported;
* speaker embeddings or equivalent voice representations where supported;
* clustering output;
* confidence or score output where supported;
* batch and chunk processing;
* provider timeout;
* bounded cancellation;
* structured failure reporting;
* raw-result retention or normalized evidence capture;
* model provenance;
* and hardware/runtime reporting.

The initial implementation may qualify one local provider.

Provider-specific values must be normalized into Phase 3 contracts or retained in explicit raw-evidence extensions.

The authoritative Phase 3 domain model must not depend on undocumented provider internals.

---

## 4.2 Speaker observations

Create stable, source-addressed speaker-observation records.

Each observation must include, at minimum:

* stable observation identifier;
* Phase 1 corpus lineage;
* selected audio-stream lineage;
* Phase 2 speech-activity references;
* relevant transcript-segment references where available;
* source-media interval;
* normalized-audio interval;
* chunk-local interval;
* overlap-ownership status;
* acoustic-evidence status;
* provider and model provenance;
* embedding reference where produced;
* quality or usability classification;
* confidence origin;
* creation timestamp;
* format and policy versions;
* and integrity fields.

Observation identifiers must be stable under identical source evidence, provider result, extraction policy, and applicable configuration.

The implementation must define which changes produce:

* artifact reuse;
* a new observation version;
* a new identifier;
* or invalidation.

---

## 4.3 Speaker turns

Create canonical speaker-turn records.

Each speaker turn must include:

* stable turn identifier;
* source interval;
* normalized-audio interval;
* one or more supporting observations;
* provisional speaker-cluster reference where available;
* turn-boundary confidence;
* speaker-assignment confidence;
* overlap status;
* transcript-segment and word references;
* interruption or continuation relationship where structurally observable;
* chunk ownership;
* provider provenance;
* and validation status.

The system must distinguish:

* single-speaker turn;
* dominant-speaker turn with background speech;
* overlapping-speaker interval;
* uncertain-speaker turn;
* unassigned speech interval;
* non-lexical vocalization;
* and unusable audio interval.

A speaker turn must not be forced to align exactly with a transcript segment.

Transcript segmentation and speaker-turn segmentation must remain independently addressable.

---

## 4.4 Speaker-change boundaries

Represent proposed speaker-change boundaries separately from turns.

Each boundary record must support:

* source timestamp or uncertainty interval;
* preceding observation or turn;
* following observation or turn;
* change confidence;
* provider basis;
* competing boundary proposals;
* whether the boundary falls inside a Phase 2 word or segment;
* whether the boundary is affected by overlap;
* and review status.

The system must not hide boundary uncertainty by rounding all changes to transcript-segment edges.

---

## 4.5 Overlapping speech

Create explicit overlap evidence.

Required behavior:

* identify intervals in which multiple voices appear active;
* record estimated number of active speakers where supported;
* preserve uncertainty about speaker count;
* link overlap intervals to supporting observations;
* associate candidate clusters when supported;
* allow partial or unknown attribution;
* represent one dominant and one secondary speaker where supported;
* and preserve provider limitations.

The system must distinguish:

* true simultaneous speech;
* rapid turn exchange;
* cross-talk;
* background speech;
* audience speech or reaction;
* replayed or broadcast speech;
* and uncertain overlap.

An overlap interval must not be flattened into one canonical speaker turn merely because one voice is louder.

---

## 4.6 Speaker embeddings and acoustic representations

Where the selected provider supports voice embeddings or an equivalent representation, store them as derived technical evidence.

Required controls:

* embeddings must be stored separately from participant identity;
* embedding format and dimensionality must be versioned;
* provider and model fingerprint must be retained;
* extraction interval and preprocessing must be recorded;
* embedding integrity must be validated;
* incompatible embedding spaces must not be compared;
* and embeddings must not be treated as portable biometric identity across unsupported conditions.

The project must document whether embeddings are stored:

* directly;
* encrypted;
* hashed where meaningful;
* referenced from a protected artifact;
* or omitted from portable exports.

The decision must account for privacy, biometric sensitivity, reproducibility, and downstream evaluation needs.

---

## 4.7 Speaker clustering

Create speaker clusters from compatible observations.

Each cluster must include:

* stable cluster identifier;
* member observations;
* member turns;
* cluster-formation method;
* provider or clustering-policy provenance;
* centroid or representative reference where applicable;
* internal similarity measurements;
* outlier measurements;
* temporal distribution;
* source coverage;
* confidence or consistency classification;
* competing cluster assignments;
* split and merge history;
* and current status.

The system must support:

* one cluster per apparent speaker where evidence permits;
* multiple clusters for one actual participant;
* one contaminated cluster containing multiple participants;
* uncertain membership;
* local unclustered observations;
* and deferred assignment.

Cluster identifiers must not be derived from participant names.

Renaming or binding a participant must not alter the underlying acoustic cluster identifier.

---

## 4.8 Cluster-consistency analysis

Evaluate the internal coherence of each proposed cluster.

Required checks include:

* pairwise or representative acoustic similarity;
* outlier observations;
* abrupt channel or quality changes;
* implausible simultaneous self-overlap;
* temporal impossibility where the same cluster appears in incompatible overlap roles;
* conflicting identity bindings;
* excessive within-cluster variance;
* unstable short observations;
* and dependence on a single weak observation.

Each cluster must receive a machine-readable consistency result.

Possible dispositions should include equivalents of:

* consistent;
* provisionally consistent;
* mixed evidence;
* likely over-merged;
* likely over-split;
* insufficient evidence;
* contaminated;
* and invalid.

A cluster-consistency result must not be presented as proof that all members belong to one human speaker.

---

## 4.9 Cluster merge and split proposals

Support bounded merge and split proposals without silently rewriting cluster history.

Merge proposals must include:

* source cluster identifiers;
* acoustic basis;
* temporal basis;
* identity-hypothesis compatibility;
* contrary evidence;
* confidence;
* and disposition.

Split proposals must include:

* target cluster identifier;
* proposed partitions;
* outlier or incompatibility evidence;
* overlap conflicts;
* identity conflicts;
* confidence;
* and disposition.

Accepted merges and splits must create successor artifacts or an explicit transformation record.

Original clusters must remain recoverable.

---

## 4.10 Identity entities

Create a minimal participant-identity representation suitable for later phases.

An identity entity may represent:

* a named individual;
* an unnamed but locally distinct participant;
* a role-defined participant;
* a moderator;
* an audience member;
* a remote or recorded voice;
* an institutional announcer;
* or an unresolved participant placeholder.

Each identity entity must include:

* stable identity identifier;
* canonical display label;
* alternate names or labels where supplied;
* identity type;
* source of identity information;
* scope;
* status;
* and provenance.

Phase 3 must not build biographical profiles, political profiles, psychological models, or credibility assessments.

Identity entities exist only to support participant attribution.

---

## 4.11 Identity hypotheses

Create identity-hypothesis records connecting clusters or observations to identity entities.

Each hypothesis must include:

* hypothesis identifier;
* target cluster, turn, or observation;
* proposed identity;
* hypothesis source;
* supporting evidence;
* contrary evidence;
* acoustic similarity where applicable;
* contextual support where explicitly permitted;
* confidence or strength;
* scope;
* competing hypotheses;
* creation process;
* and disposition.

Permitted hypothesis sources may include:

* reference-voice comparison;
* trusted external roster or event metadata;
* transcript self-identification;
* moderator introduction;
* manual reviewer proposal;
* imported annotation;
* and later audiovisual evidence.

The implementation must distinguish between:

* acoustic support;
* contextual support;
* documentary support;
* manual assertion;
* and confirmed binding.

These must not be collapsed into one undifferentiated confidence value.

---

## 4.12 Reference-voice enrollment

Implement bounded reference-voice enrollment.

A reference enrollment must include:

* reference identifier;
* proposed identity;
* source and licensing status;
* consent or lawful-use status where recorded;
* recording provenance;
* source interval;
* audio-quality assessment;
* speech duration;
* contamination assessment;
* extraction provider;
* embedding or representation reference;
* enrollment scope;
* expiration or revocation status where applicable;
* and validation result.

The system must support:

* one or more references per identity;
* incompatible or low-quality references;
* rejected enrollment;
* revoked enrollment;
* conflicting references;
* and reference replacement without erasing history.

Reference material must not be treated as valid merely because a file carries a person’s name.

Enrollment must require explicit provenance and validation.

---

## 4.13 Reference-voice comparison

Compare eligible cluster or observation evidence against compatible enrolled references.

Each comparison must record:

* target observation or cluster;
* reference enrollment;
* compatible model space;
* comparison score;
* threshold policy;
* cohort or calibration context where available;
* quality conditions;
* supporting and contrary evidence;
* uncertainty;
* and result classification.

Result classifications should include equivalents of:

* supports hypothesis;
* weakly supports hypothesis;
* inconclusive;
* weakly contradicts hypothesis;
* contradicts hypothesis;
* and comparison invalid.

The system must not convert a similarity score directly into a definitive identity binding.

Where calibration is unavailable, the report must say so explicitly.

Scores from different models, model versions, or embedding spaces must not be compared as though they share one scale.

---

## 4.14 Manual identity binding

Implement append-only manual binding records.

Manual actions must support:

* bind cluster to identity;
* bind observation or turn to identity;
* reject proposed identity;
* mark identity unknown;
* revise a prior binding;
* limit binding scope;
* merge identity placeholders;
* split an incorrectly unified identity;
* and restore an earlier binding state.

Each manual binding must record:

* binding identifier;
* target artifact;
* identity;
* scope;
* action;
* predecessor binding where applicable;
* author;
* timestamp;
* rationale;
* supporting evidence references;
* contrary evidence acknowledged;
* confidence or reviewer certainty;
* and resulting identity-view version.

Manual binding must not modify the original diarization result.

A manual label must be visibly distinguishable from a model-generated hypothesis.

---

## 4.15 Identity-view assembly

Assemble deterministic participant-assignment views from stored artifacts.

Required views:

* raw provider diarization;
* canonical machine diarization;
* cluster-consistency view;
* unresolved-speaker view;
* identity-hypothesis view;
* reference-comparison view;
* manually reviewed identity view;
* and binding-history view.

The system must not silently resolve:

* conflicting bindings;
* overlapping identities;
* one cluster bound to incompatible identities within the same scope;
* one identity assigned impossibly to simultaneous independent speakers;
* unresolved split or merge proposals;
* or incompatible reference comparisons.

Such conditions must produce explicit validation findings.

---

## 4.16 Transcript integration

Phase 3 must integrate with Phase 2 without rewriting transcript evidence.

Required behavior:

* associate speaker turns with relevant transcript segments and words;
* permit one transcript segment to span multiple speaker turns;
* permit one speaker turn to span multiple transcript segments;
* support unattributed transcript content;
* support multiple candidate speakers for an interval;
* preserve Phase 2 correction lineage;
* and make clear whether speaker attribution applies to the machine transcript, corrected transcript, or both.

Speaker labels in subtitle or transcript views must be generated from a declared Phase 3 identity-view version.

They must not be inserted into the authoritative Phase 2 transcript artifact.

---

## 4.17 Participant-labeled subtitle derivatives

Phase 3 may extend subtitle export to produce optional participant-labeled derivatives.

Required conditions:

* source transcript version must be declared;
* identity-view version must be declared;
* unresolved speakers must remain explicitly unknown or cluster-labeled;
* conflicting bindings must block authoritative labeled export;
* label-rounding or cue-splitting rules must be recorded;
* overlapping speakers must not be falsely serialized without disclosure;
* and a companion manifest must preserve source and identity lineage.

Participant-labeled subtitles remain presentation derivatives.

They are not authoritative identity records.

---

## 4.18 Cache, resume, and recovery

Phase 3 must extend prior operational guarantees.

Required behavior:

* cache diarization-provider output separately from normalized observations;
* cache speaker embeddings separately from cluster assignments;
* cache cluster results separately from identity hypotheses;
* cache reference enrollments and comparisons with complete model lineage;
* validate artifacts before reuse;
* quarantine corrupt entries;
* invalidate only affected downstream stages where practical;
* resume after persisted stage boundaries;
* recover from interrupted observation extraction, clustering, comparison, binding assembly, and export;
* and avoid provider reinvocation where valid evidence already exists.

A change to a participant name must not force acoustic reprocessing.

A change to clustering policy must not force speech transcription unless the transcription evidence itself is invalidated.

A change to the embedding model must invalidate incompatible embeddings, clusters dependent upon them, and reference comparisons, but not unrelated Phase 1 or Phase 2 artifacts.

## 6. Processing sequence

Implementation should proceed in the following order.

### Stage 1: Contracts and evidence boundaries

* Define Phase 3 contracts.
* Define diarization-provider abstraction.
* Define observation, turn, overlap, cluster, identity, and binding semantics.
* Define identifier and versioning rules.
* Define cache and invalidation boundaries.
* Define privacy and export policy for voice embeddings.

### Stage 2: Speaker observations and turns

* Process Phase 2 speech intervals.
* Produce speaker observations.
* Produce provisional turns.
* Preserve uncertain boundaries.
* Map turns to source and transcript evidence.
* Reconcile chunk overlap ownership.

### Stage 3: Overlap representation

* Detect or normalize overlapping-speech evidence.
* Produce overlap intervals.
* Associate candidate observations and clusters.
* Preserve unknown speaker count where necessary.
* Validate temporal consistency.

### Stage 4: Speaker clustering

* Generate or normalize acoustic representations.
* Construct provisional clusters.
* Preserve unclustered observations.
* Produce cluster-membership evidence.
* Validate simultaneous-self conflicts and other temporal impossibilities.

### Stage 5: Cluster-consistency analysis

* Measure internal coherence.
* Identify likely over-merges and over-splits.
* Produce outlier records.
* Generate bounded merge and split proposals.
* Do not silently apply proposals.

### Stage 6: Identity foundations

* Create participant-identity entities.
* Import or create event rosters where available.
* Generate bounded identity hypotheses.
* Preserve source and scope for each hypothesis.

### Stage 7: Reference-voice enrollment and comparison

* Validate reference material.
* Enroll compatible reference voices.
* Compare eligible observations and clusters.
* Preserve scores, calibration limits, and alternative explanations.
* Produce support, contradiction, or inconclusive results.

### Stage 8: Manual identity binding

* Implement append-only binding records.
* Create reviewed identity views.
* Support revisions, scope limits, rejection, and restoration.
* Validate conflicts and impossible assignments.

### Stage 9: Transcript and subtitle integration

* Render speaker-labeled transcript views.
* Produce optional labeled WebVTT and SRT derivatives.
* Preserve unresolved and overlapping assignments.
* Produce export manifests.

### Stage 10: Evaluation and qualification

* Evaluate turns, boundaries, overlap, clustering, and identity foundations.
* Exercise unknown, conflict, merge, split, and manual-binding cases.
* Test cache, recovery, corruption, and provider failures.
* Produce checked-in qualification evidence.

## 9. Confidence requirements

Phase 3 must not collapse confidence into one universal score.

At minimum, distinguish:

* speech-presence confidence inherited from Phase 2;
* speaker-change confidence;
* turn-boundary confidence;
* observation-usability confidence;
* cluster-membership confidence;
* cluster-consistency confidence;
* overlap confidence;
* speaker-count confidence;
* acoustic identity-support strength;
* contextual identity-support strength;
* reference-comparison validity;
* manual-review certainty;
* and binding status.

Where a provider does not supply a meaningful confidence value, the field must remain unavailable or explicitly derived.

Derived confidence must record:

* derivation method;
* input evidence;
* threshold policy;
* and limitations.

Scores from different models or embedding spaces must not be treated as interchangeable.

## 10. Privacy and biometric safeguards

Because voice representations may constitute sensitive biometric evidence, Phase 3 must document and enforce a storage and export policy.

At minimum:

* raw reference audio must retain provenance and access policy;
* reference voices must not be enrolled silently;
* embeddings must not be exported by default unless the export policy explicitly permits them;
* portable reports should use references or summaries where possible;
* deletion or revocation of enrolled references must be representable;
* manual identity binding must not imply consent to biometric enrollment;
* identity comparison must be bounded to declared project purposes;
* and logs must not unnecessarily reproduce private reference audio or embedding contents.

The phase need not implement a complete legal-compliance system, but it must not design as though voice identity evidence were ordinary non-sensitive metadata.

## 15. Long-recording requirements

Phase 3 must qualify operation on a recording longer than two hours.

The long-recording proof must demonstrate:

* bounded memory;
* Phase 1 chunk and overlap ownership;
* Phase 2 speech-interval reuse;
* stable speaker-observation creation;
* chunk-local provider invocation;
* cross-chunk cluster continuity;
* no duplicated canonical turns;
* interruption and resume;
* cache replay;
* final cluster assembly;
* identity-view assembly;
* and complete integrity validation.

A synthetic marker provider may qualify mechanics, but it must not be used to claim real diarization or speaker-recognition accuracy.

At least one non-synthetic, multi-speaker recording should be used for a limited operational demonstration, even if it is not sufficient for general quality claims.

---

## 16. Regression requirements

Phase 3 must preserve all Phase 0 through Phase 2 guarantees.

The regression suite must demonstrate that Phase 3 does not:

* alter source audiovisual files;
* alter Phase 1 normalized audio;
* alter Phase 1 stream or timeline decisions;
* alter Phase 2 speech-activity records;
* alter Phase 2 transcript text or corrections;
* convert clusters into persons without a recorded hypothesis or binding;
* overwrite raw provider results;
* erase unknown or conflicting assignments;
* introduce argument analysis;
* introduce factual adjudication;
* introduce credibility analysis;
* introduce political classification;
* introduce psychological inference;
* introduce judgment;
* or score the truthfulness, quality, or worth of a participant.

Speaker-labeled transcript views must remain derived views over unchanged Phase 2 evidence.

---

## 17. Explicit exclusions

Phase 3 must not implement:

* face recognition;
* face-to-voice association;
* lip-motion active-speaker detection;
* global participant identity across unrelated corpora without explicit enrollment and scope;
* covert speaker identification;
* inferred demographic classification from voice;
* age, race, ethnicity, sex, gender, health, disability, nationality, or emotional-state classification from voice;
* personality inference;
* deception detection;
* credibility scoring;
* truthfulness scoring;
* political-position inference;
* argument extraction;
* claim adjudication;
* rhetorical evaluation;
* dominance scoring;
* interruption blame;
* debate winner selection;
* or final legal identity determination.

The phase may create foundations later consumed by audiovisual participant-resolution work, but it must not silently perform those later functions.

## 20. Exit gates

Phase 3 is complete only when all of the following gates pass.

### Gate 1: Source-addressed observations

Every canonical speaker observation maps validly to the Phase 1 corpus and relevant Phase 2 speech evidence.

### Gate 2: Speaker turns

The system produces stable, confidence-bearing speaker turns without forcing identity.

### Gate 3: Speaker-change evidence

Speaker-change boundaries are explicit, source-addressed, and capable of representing temporal uncertainty.

### Gate 4: Overlap

Simultaneous or potentially simultaneous speech is represented explicitly and is not flattened into false single-speaker certainty.

### Gate 5: Speaker clustering

Compatible observations can be grouped into provisional clusters with preserved membership evidence and unknown assignments.

### Gate 6: Cluster consistency

Each cluster receives a machine-readable consistency result capable of exposing over-merges, over-splits, contamination, and insufficient evidence.

### Gate 7: Merge and split history

Cluster merge and split proposals are bounded, reviewable, and append-only; original clusters remain recoverable.

### Gate 8: Identity hypotheses

Clusters and observations may carry bounded identity hypotheses with explicit supporting, contrary, and competing evidence.

### Gate 9: Reference-voice enrollment

Reference voices can be enrolled only with provenance, validation, model lineage, scope, and privacy handling.

### Gate 10: Reference comparison

Compatible references and target evidence can be compared without converting similarity into automatic identity certainty.

### Gate 11: Manual identity binding

Human reviewers can bind, reject, revise, limit, and restore identity assignments through attributable append-only records.

### Gate 12: Unknown and conflict

Unidentified speakers, weak clusters, conflicting hypotheses, and incompatible bindings remain explicit rather than being forced into names.

### Gate 13: Transcript integration

Speaker assignments can be rendered over Phase 2 transcript evidence without modifying the authoritative transcript artifacts.

### Gate 14: Evaluation

Turn segmentation, speaker confusion, overlap, clustering, and identity-foundation behavior are measured against controlled references.

### Gate 15: Cache and recovery

Validated outputs are safely reused, affected artifacts are selectively invalidated, corrupted entries are quarantined, and processing resumes without duplicated or lost evidence.

### Gate 16: Integrity

Malformed, incompatible, impossible, or conflicting Phase 3 artifacts are detected and refused or explicitly degraded.

### Gate 17: Long-recording operation

A recording longer than two hours is processed with bounded memory, cross-chunk continuity, resumability, cache replay, and valid final assembly.

### Gate 18: Regression and boundary

All prior-phase tests pass, and no face identification, argument analysis, factual adjudication, credibility scoring, psychological inference, or participant judgment is introduced.

---

## 21. Success definition

Phase 3 succeeds when Ratiocinatus can take a valid Phase 2 transcript-evidence package and produce a portable, integrity-checked participant-speech evidence package that:

* identifies provisional speaker turns;
* represents uncertain speaker changes;
* preserves overlapping speech;
* groups compatible voice observations into provisional clusters;
* measures cluster consistency;
* retains over-merge and over-split uncertainty;
* supports bounded identity hypotheses;
* supports validated reference-voice enrollment and comparison;
* supports append-only manual identity binding;
* preserves unknown and conflicting states;
* renders participant-labeled transcript and subtitle derivatives;
* survives interruption and recovery;
* supports controlled diarization evaluation;
* and remains fully traceable to the underlying audiovisual source.

The result must be suitable for later active-speaker, participant-resolution, discourse, argument, and adjudication phases without representing a voice cluster or similarity score as definitive proof of human identity.
